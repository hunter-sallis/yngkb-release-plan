Editorial playlist pitching does not happen inside DistroKid. DistroKid is the pipe that delivers the song; the pitch itself is made in Spotify for Artists, from the song's entry under Upcoming. Because this is a first release there is no Spotify for Artists profile yet, so the plan below is really three things in order: upload early enough, claim the profile while the song is still unreleased, then pitch before the seven-day cut-off.

Where the pitch actually lives

Delivery only

DistroKid

Takes the audio, artwork and metadata and sends them to Spotify and the other stores. Its release-date field is where the timing is set, and its Looker-Upper is how you get the artist URI for the profile claim. It has no editorial pitch form. Its own playlist tools (Wheel of Playlist, Playlist Spotlight, Playlister) only work on songs that are already live, so they are for after release.

Where you pitch

Spotify for Artists

artists.spotify.com, or the Spotify for Artists app. Once the profile is claimed, the unreleased song appears under Music → Upcoming with a pitch form: genre, mood, instrumentation, the story behind the song, and your promotion plan. One song can be in the pitch queue at a time, and a song stops being pitchable the moment it goes live.

Key findings

8 findings · select one to read it

The video can premiere on Friday 11 September 2026 as planned; nothing about it touches the Spotify pitch. The audio is different. A release the week after would need a pitch submitted seven days before it — before the song has even been delivered, before the artist URI exists and before there is a Spotify for Artists profile to pitch from. Working forward instead: finish the upload by Thu 10 Sep, expect the URI around Sun 13 Sep, claim the profile by Tue 15 Sep, and pitch immediately; the hard deadline for a Fri 2 Oct release is Fri 25 Sep. That is 22 days of lead time, under DistroKid's four-week guidance for a first-release profile claim but inside its three-week editorial minimum, so the upload has to be finished this week for it to hold. If the upload slips, move the release one Friday later rather than skipping the pitch.

DistroVid is DistroKid's separate music-video service: $99 a year for unlimited videos on one artist slot, not included in the Musician, Musician Plus or Ultimate plans, and not active on this account. It delivers to Spotify, Apple Music, TIDAL, Vevo, Boomplay and, per the Help Center, Amazon Music. Spotify only accepts a video whose audio single is already distributed by DistroKid with the artist name and title matching exactly, so the video cannot lead the audio there. DistroKid asks for four weeks' lead for a fixed video date and says stores take one to five days after approval, sometimes a couple of weeks. YouTube is reached through a new Vevo channel rather than the artist's own; a self-uploaded YouTube copy has to be made private first or it duplicates, and its views do not transfer. Videos must be full-length, one song, 1080p or better, with no watermarks, text, handles, release dates or promo overlays. Content ID is a different switch: it comes from the Social Media Pack extra on the audio release, not from DistroVid, and only if every sound in the recording is owned outright.

The DistroKid dashboard shows no releases, and Spotify only creates an artist page once a distributor has delivered a release under that name. The editorial pitch form lives inside a claimed Spotify for Artists profile, so until the song is delivered and the profile is claimed there is nowhere to pitch. DistroKid documents the pre-release route: pull the artist URI from its Looker-Upper after delivery, then claim at artists.spotify.com/claim.

DistroKid's on-page note says a release date at least one week out; its editorial guidance says three weeks, and its first-release claim guidance says four. The four-week figure is the one that leaves room for delivery, ingestion, the profile claim and the pitch with a buffer. Your account is on Musician Plus, which includes custom future release dates, so the calendar above is reachable as it stands.

Spotify allows a single pitch for each song, one unreleased song in the pitch queue at a time, and closes pitching the moment a song is released. Anyone with Admin or Editor access can edit the pitch up to release day, though there is no guarantee editors see the edits. Songs you appear on as a featured artist, and compilations, cannot be pitched.

Wheel of Playlist, Playlist Spotlight and Playlister sit under Goodies → Get heard now in the grid menu at the top right of the dashboard. Spotlight only lists songs currently live in Spotify, and Wheel requires a live release too, so neither substitutes for the editorial pitch; they add on top of it once the song is out. Playlister, the curator contact search, is Ultimate plan only.

Apple Music for Artists can be claimed once the song has been live for five business days, and gives stats, artwork and bio control, but there is no pitch form for independent artists — Apple placements follow from a complete profile and momentum. Amazon Music for Artists does include playlist pitching once claimed via DistroKid. TIDAL Artist Home and the YouTube Official Artist Channel are claimable through DistroKid's Special Access menu after release.

Spotify adds any song pitched at least seven days before release to the Release Radar playlists of the artist's followers automatically. Editorial placement is never guaranteed, but this part is, which is why building a follower count on the new profile between the claim and release day is worth the time.

What happens next

  1. Finish the upload this week, with the release date set to Fri 2 Oct

    The release date field is on the upload form you already have open. DistroKid's on-page note says one week is the minimum; that is far too tight for what follows. Set Friday 2 October 2026 (or a later Friday), which gives delivery, Spotify ingestion, the profile claim and the pitch each their own room. Upload by Thu 10 Sep. Custom future release dates need the Musician Plus or Ultimate plan; if the date picker will not let you choose a future date, that is the reason, and the Upgrade button in the top bar is the fix.

    DistroKid — upload form DistroKid — setting a future release date DistroKid — pitching for Spotify editorial

    While you are on the form: fill in songwriter and producer credits, the lyrics, and the correct primary artist name exactly as you want it to appear. The artist name string on this first upload is what creates the Spotify artist page you are about to claim.

    About the video. Put it on a YouTube channel named exactly after the artist and keep it as a full upload rather than a Short — that same channel becomes the Official Artist Channel after release (step 7). Spotify's video pitching is a separate form that only covers videos delivered by a distributor or uploaded inside Spotify for Artists; the song pitch in step 4 is the one that matters here.

  2. The video: your own YouTube channel now, DistroVid after the song is live

    DistroKid does distribute music videos, through DistroVid — a separate subscription at $99 a year for unlimited videos on one artist slot. It is not a tier of the DistroKid plan and no upgrade is involved; DistroKid's Help Center says a free DistroKid account is all it needs. It sends the video to Spotify, Apple Music, TIDAL, Vevo and Boomplay (the Help Center also lists Amazon Music), and you keep 100% of what those services pay. It is not on this account today.

    It does not change Friday. Spotify only accepts a video whose audio single is already distributed by DistroKid under an exactly matching artist name and title, so the video cannot go out ahead of the song there, and DistroKid asks for four weeks' lead when a video has to land on a fixed date. Friday's premiere is a straight upload to a YouTube channel named YNG KB. DistroVid is the step after Fri 2 Oct.

    How the DistroVid upload works

    1. Subscribe at distrokid.com/distrovid ("Pay and start uploading"). One artist slot is included; more can be added.
    2. Upload the file. .mp4 or .mov · H.264 or ProRes 422 · 1920×1080 up to 3840×2160 · progressive · 23.976, 24, 25 or 30 fps · variable bit rate · stereo audio at 44.1 kHz or higher · at least 30 seconds · under 30 GB. No HEVC/H.265.
    3. Tick the stores at the top of the form: Spotify, Apple Music, TIDAL, Vevo, Boomplay, and the optional DistroVid Gallery. Spotify reviews every video for "premium video quality" and can decline it.
    4. Vevo channel section. Choose "No – there is no existing VEVO channel" and DistroKid requests one with the first upload, named from the artist name (16 characters or fewer, no punctuation — so YNGKBVEVO). If you tick Vevo, set your own YouTube copy to private first: otherwise the video appears twice on YouTube, and the views on your original do not carry over.
    5. Add credits and collaborators, then submit. Stores take one to five days after approval, sometimes a couple of weeks. There is no edit button afterwards: a wrong file means delete and re-upload, and metadata fixes go through support.
    6. Later additions. A video already delivered can be sent to more stores from Videos → your video → + Add more platforms.

    Content ID — where the protection actually comes from

    DistroVid does not put the song into YouTube Content ID. DistroKid's Content ID access is a separate, per-release extra called Social Media Pack, ticked under Extras on the audio upload form (or added later from the release page under Add Extras). Once it is on, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and Facebook fingerprint the recording and pay you when it turns up in anyone's video — fan edits, reaction clips, your own uploads — with DistroKid keeping 20% of that share. DistroKid's own Vevo page draws the line plainly: Vevo pays for views of the Vevo video; YouTube ad money from the audio in any video only flows if Social Media Pack is on the release.

    Do it on this week's upload, so the recording is registered before the video and the song start circulating, and before anyone else's upload of it can be treated as the original.

    One condition that matters for a rap record. YouTube only accepts a recording into Content ID if you own 100% of the sounds — no purchased or leased beats built from loops, sample packs or stock kits, and no one else (a producer, a beat store) registering the same audio. If the beat is not fully original or the producer already runs Content ID on it, the release is not eligible and opting in creates a conflict rather than protection. Confirm this with the producer before ticking the box.

    If Content ID ever claims your own YouTube upload, that is the system working: the claim routes the ad money to you through DistroKid. To keep a specific video clean instead, add it to DistroKid's YouTube Allowlist.

    United States on Spotify. A DistroVid video goes live on Spotify worldwide on delivery except in the U.S., where Spotify requires the composition to be fully registered at The MLC (100% of shares) and every publisher opted into Spotify's audiovisual licence through the Harry Fox Agency. That is publishing administration — the part Keyman handles — and it is another reason the song's registrations should be complete before the video is sent.

  3. Pull the Spotify artist URI once the song is delivered

    After DistroKid shows the release as sent to stores, open the Spotify Looker-Upper, choose the release from the dropdown and click What's the Spotify URI?. The result lists album, track and artist URIs; the one you need is under Artist. If the page says the release has not been ingested yet, that is normal — Spotify generally needs a day or so. Check again the next day rather than re-uploading anything.

    DistroKid — Spotify Looker-Upper DistroKid — claiming your profile before a first release

  4. Claim the Spotify for Artists profile with that URI

    Go to the Spotify for Artists claim page, choose Artist or manager, and sign in with a Spotify account (a free one is enough). Paste the artist URI into the search box on the next screen and complete the claim. If the search returns nothing within a day or two of delivery, Spotify has not built the artist page yet — try again the following day. Once approved, the unreleased song appears under Music → Upcoming.

    Spotify for Artists — claim your profile DistroKid — instant access after the song is live

    Add us to the team as an Editor once you are in, so the pitch can be drafted and refined without passing your login around. Anyone with Admin or Editor access can edit the pitch up to release day.

  5. Pitch the song — once, and early

    In Spotify for Artists open Music → Upcoming, choose the song and complete the pitch form. Spotify's floor is seven days before release, which for this plan is Fri 25 Sep; pitching earlier gives editors more listening time and costs nothing. You get one pitch per song and only one unreleased song in the queue at a time, so this is the one to get right: pick the genre and sub-genre honestly, tag mood and instrumentation, write the story behind the record in a few sentences, and describe the promotion you actually have planned. Submitting at least seven days out also places the song in your followers' Release Radar automatically.

  6. Release day and the week after

    On Fri 2 Oct check Music → Playlists in Spotify for Artists to see whether editors placed the song; they sometimes choose a different track than the one pitched, and no pitch is guaranteed. From this point DistroKid's own playlist tools open up, because they require the song to be live on Spotify.

    • Wheel of Playlist — spin for a spot on DistroKid's Spotify playlist. distrokid.com/wheel
    • Playlist Spotlight — a weekly head-to-head fan vote; winners land on DistroKid genre playlists. One song per artist at a time, votes reset weekly, you get a share link for fans. distrokid.com/spotlight/submit
    • Playlister — curator contact search, Ultimate plan only. Use it for independent playlists, never for anyone selling placements.
    Do not pay for playlist placement. Spotify treats bought placements and the streams they generate as artificial streaming; DistroKid relays those notices and the song can be pulled. Anything that promises a guaranteed number of streams is the thing to avoid.

  7. Claim the other artist profiles once the song is live

    These all sit behind DistroKid's grid menu under Special Access, and each one needs the song to be live first. None of them has a self-serve editorial pitch form for independent artists — Apple, in particular, does not — so these are about owning the profile, not pitching.

    • Apple Music for Artists — claimable once the song has been live at least five business days (Fri 9 Oct). Paste the Apple Music artist link, choose your role, and sign in with your DistroKid account for faster verification. artists.apple.com
    • Amazon Music for Artists — Get Started → Claim an Artist → Connect via Distributor → DistroKid. Includes Amazon's own playlist pitching once claimed. artists.amazon.com
    • TIDAL Artist Home — search your artist name on tidal.com, open the profile, choose claim, verify through DistroKid. artists.tidal.com
    • YouTube Official Artist Channel — needs a YouTube channel named after the artist with at least one real upload (Shorts do not count), and the release live on YouTube Music. The claim cannot be undone, so choose the channel carefully. Allow up to six weeks. distrokid.com/YouTubeOfficialArtistChannels
Why the order matters. Every step here is gated by the one before it: the artist URI does not exist until DistroKid has delivered the song, the profile cannot be claimed without the URI, and the pitch form only appears inside a claimed profile while the song is still unreleased. Compressing the release date squeezes the pitch out of the calendar entirely, and a song that goes live before it is pitched can never be pitched.
